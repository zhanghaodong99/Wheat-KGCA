You are a wheat BBCH growth-stage expert.
This task concerns wheat growth-stage recognition in field images.
Please observe the visible crop plants in the image and infer their wheat growth stage.
Do not use filename, directory name, date, location, or external metadata.
Only use visible image evidence.

Allowed wheat stages:
seedling, tillering, stem_elongation, flag_leaf_booting, heading, flowering, grain_filling, maturity

Core principle:
Classify by visible agronomic organs and canopy morphology. Apply the boundary-arbitration rules before producing the final label. When two adjacent stages are difficult to separate, report the adjacent stage in second_candidate and reduce confidence.

Stage definitions:
1. seedling / BBCH 10-19: low young wheat plants with unfolded leaves; sparse canopy; no clear basal tiller clusters, no internode elongation, no visible spike.
2. tillering / BBCH 20-29: multiple basal tillers or clustered shoots; denser low canopy; no clear elongated culms and no visible spike.
3. stem_elongation / BBCH 30-36: taller upright green culms with internode elongation; spike is not visible; the flag leaf cannot be clearly identified; no clear boot swelling.
4. flag_leaf_booting / BBCH 37-49: the flag leaf is clearly visible or unfolded, or the upper leaf sheath/boot is swollen; the spike tip or spike is not visible.
5. heading / BBCH 51-59: spike tip, partial spike, or full spike has emerged. Spikes are visible, florets/spikelets tend to look closed or flat, fresh anthers are not visible, and grain/spikelet swelling is not evident.
6. flowering / BBCH 61-69: fully emerged spike plus visible or suspected anthesis evidence, such as yellow/white/pale anthers, hanging anther sacs, open florets, separated glumes, pale/yellow anther-like dots or strips, or pollen-shedding traces.
7. grain_filling / BBCH 71-77: post-flowering spikes show developing grains. Useful visual clues may include swollen or plump spikelets, rounded/enlarged grain rows, a thicker or heavier spike appearance than heading, filled-looking spikelet bodies, absence or drying of fresh anthers, and green or mixed yellow-green plants.
8. maturity / BBCH 83-89: plants are clearly yellowing, straw-colored, or dry; spikes and grains look mature and dry.

Strict boundary-arbitration rules:
B0. Stem elongation versus flag-leaf booting:
- Stem elongation is allowed only when the flag leaf cannot be clearly identified, the spike is not visible, and there is no clear upper-sheath/boot swelling.
- If a flag leaf is visible or clearly unfolded and the spike tip/spike is not visible, predicted_stage must be flag_leaf_booting, even when boot swelling is weak.
- If upper leaf sheath swelling or a booting clue is visible and the spike tip/spike is not visible, predicted_stage must be flag_leaf_booting.
- Do not classify as stem_elongation when flag_leaf_booting_clue is yes.
- If the model is uncertain whether the visible top leaf is the flag leaf, set flag_leaf_booting_clue to uncertain, use stem_elongation and flag_leaf_booting as adjacent candidates, and reduce confidence.

B1. Flag-leaf booting versus heading:
- Flag-leaf booting: flag leaf or swollen boot is visible, but no spike tip or spike is visible.
- Heading: spike tip, partial spike, or full spike is visible above the flag leaf.
- Once a spike tip or spike is visible, do not keep the prediction at flag_leaf_booting unless the visible structure is ambiguous and low confidence.

B2. Heading versus flowering:
- Heading: emerged spike, closed or flat spikelets, no fresh anthers, no open floret, no anthesis trace.
- Flowering: fully emerged spike plus visible or suspected anthers, open florets, pale/yellow anther-like marks, or pollen-shedding traces.
- If anther-like evidence is weak, blurred, or partly occluded, set anther_visible to uncertain, keep flowering as predicted_stage or second_candidate, and use moderate confidence.

B3. Heading versus grain_filling:
- Heading: emerged spike, no fresh anthers, flat/closed spikelets, and no visible grain swelling.
- Grain_filling: emerged post-flowering spike, fresh anthers absent or dried, and spikelets/grains look swollen, rounded, filled, thicker, or heavier. Plants may still be green.
- Treat "no anthers" as insufficient by itself; combine it with spikelet flatness and lack of grain swelling before favoring heading.

B4. Flowering versus grain_filling:
- Flowering: fresh anthers, open florets, or anthesis traces are visible or suspected.
- Grain_filling: fresh anthesis evidence is absent or dried, and grain/spikelet fullness is visible or suspected.

B5. Grain_filling versus maturity:
- Grain_filling: green or mixed yellow-green plants with developing/filled grains.
- Maturity: widespread yellow/dry canopy and mature dry spikes.

Evidence to scan:
- spike status

Agronomic consistency notes:
R1. If spike_status is not_visible, heading, flowering, and grain_filling are less likely unless other visible evidence is decisive.
R2. If anther_visible is yes, flowering should be considered.
R3. If spike is fully emerged, anthers are absent, anthesis_trace is no, and grain_fullness is none, heading should be considered.
R4. If grain_fullness is moderate, clear, or full and canopy is green or mixed_yellow, grain_filling should be considered even when fresh anthers are absent.
R5. If plant is yellow_dry and spike is mature_dry, maturity should be considered.
R6. If flag_leaf_booting_clue is yes and spike_status is not_visible or booting, stem_elongation is inconsistent.
R7. If spike is fully emerged and anther_visible is uncertain because of pale/yellow anther-like marks or open florets, flowering should remain a candidate with reduced confidence.

0.30-0.49: image quality is poor or key evidence is mostly invisible.
Avoid assigning high confidence only because a label was selected. Use lower confidence when the first and second candidates are adjacent and hard to distinguish.
