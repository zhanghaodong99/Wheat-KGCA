Allowed wheat stages:

Stage definitions:
1. seedling / BBCH 10-19: low wheat plants with unfolded leaves; no obvious tillering cluster, no internode elongation, and no visible spike.
2. tillering / BBCH 20-29: multiple basal tillers and clustered shoots; no clear internode elongation and no visible spike.
3. stem_elongation / BBCH 30-36: upright and taller culms with internode elongation; the spike is not visible.
4. flag_leaf_booting / BBCH 37-49: flag leaf is visible or the leaf sheath is swollen; the spike is still hidden in the boot or only barely indicated.
5. heading / BBCH 51-59: the spike tip to the full spike has emerged. Spikes are visible above the flag leaf, but florets appear closed and there is no visible anther, pollen-shedding clue, or flowering trace.
6. flowering / BBCH 61-69: the spike is usually fully emerged, and flowering evidence appears on the spike. Key clues include visible yellow, white, or pale anthers extruding from florets; small hanging or protruding anther sacs on spikelet sides; open florets or separated glumes; pale/yellow dots or strips along spikelets that look like anthers; or clear pollen-shedding/anthesis traces. If the full spike is emerged and these flowering clues are visible or strongly suspected but not perfectly sharp, consider flowering and lower confidence instead of forcing heading.
7. grain_filling / BBCH 71-77: post-flowering spikes show developing or filled grains; spikelets look plumper or heavier; anthers are usually absent or dried; plants are still green or mixed yellow-green.
8. maturity / BBCH 83-89: plants are clearly yellowing or dry; spike and grains are mature and dry.

Important boundary rules:
B1. Heading versus flowering:
- Heading: emerged spike, closed-looking spikelets, no anther-like yellow/white/pale protrusions, no open floret or pollen-shedding trace.
- Flowering: fully emerged spike plus visible or strongly suspected anthesis evidence, especially anthers, hanging sacs, open florets, or pale/yellow anther-like marks along spikelets.
- If anthers are not clearly sharp but there are suspicious pale/yellow dots, strips, or protrusions on fully emerged spikes, set anther_visible to uncertain, choose flowering as predicted_stage or second_candidate, and use moderate confidence.

B2. Flowering versus grain_filling:
- Flowering: anthesis evidence is present or suspected; spikes remain relatively fresh and not obviously filled.
- Grain_filling: grains or spikelets look plump/full, flowering traces are absent or dried, and the crop is green to mixed yellow-green.

B3. Do not hallucinate flowering:
- Do not mark anther_visible as yes unless anther-like structures are visible.
- If flowering evidence is weak, use anther_visible = uncertain and explain the uncertainty.
- Do not classify as flowering only because the image label might be flowering; rely on visible evidence.
